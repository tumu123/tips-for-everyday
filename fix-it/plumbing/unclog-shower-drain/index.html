How to Fix a Clogged Shower Drain

Fix It Plumbing Published October 17, 2026 6 min read

A shower drain clogs with something different from a kitchen sink, and that difference determines which method works.

A shower clog is hair bound together with soap scum. Hair alone washes through; soap scum alone is soft. Together they form a mat that catches more hair, dead skin, and product residue until the pipe closes up.

Soap scum is the insoluble residue left when soap meets hard water — calcium and magnesium salts of fatty acids. It does not dissolve in water, which is why it accumulates rather than washing away.

This explains the two things people get wrong:

  1. Chemical drain cleaner rarely works on a shower clog. Hair is protein, and the products that dissolve it need long contact times and are hazardous to leave in a bathroom. Most of the time the cleaner drains past the clog without clearing it — and then you reach in by hand into a pipe full of chemicals.
  2. Mechanical removal is the correct approach. A barbed plastic strip pulls the mat out in one pass, in about five minutes, with no chemicals.

Step 0: Confirm It Is the Shower, Not the Main Line

This determines whether you fix it yourself.

  1. Run water in other fixtures. If the sink, toilet, and tub are all slow or backing up, the problem is downstream — likely the main line, which is a plumber’s job.
  2. If only the shower is slow, the clog is local to that drain and you can usually clear it.
  3. Check whether water backs up elsewhere when the shower runs. If running the shower backs up the sink, the blockage is past where the two lines join.
  4. Do not use any drain cleaner until you know it is a local clog. Adding chemicals to a main-line blockage makes the eventual professional job more hazardous.

Step 1: Remove the Cover and Pull Out What You Can Reach

  1. Remove the drain cover. Most are held by a screw in the centre, or they lift or unscrew off.
  2. Put on gloves. This is the least pleasant part of the job and the one people most want to skip — do it anyway.
  3. Look into the drain with a light. In many cases the clog is visible and within reach.
  4. Use needle-nose pliers or a bent wire hook to pull out whatever you can see.
  5. You will often remove most of the clog this way. Start here before reaching for tools.

If your shower has a crossbar or a strainer basket under the cover, clear that first — it catches hair by design and is frequently the entire problem.

Step 2: Use a Barbed Plastic Strip

This is the single most effective tool for a shower clog, and it costs very little.

  1. Insert the strip straight down into the drain, pushing gently until it meets resistance or bottoms out.
  2. Do not force it through a resistance you cannot identify. It is designed to go into the trap, not past a solid blockage.
  3. Slowly pull it back out. The barbs catch hair and drag it out with the tool.
  4. Expect it to come out loaded. This is normal and it is the point.
  5. Repeat several times until the strip comes back with little or nothing on it.
  6. Dispose of the debris in the trash, not back down the drain.

Why this beats a plunger for hair: a plunger pushes the mat further along and compacts it. A strip removes it, which is the difference between clearing the symptom and clearing the cause.

Step 3: Try a Plunger if the Strip Is Not Enough

  1. Remove the drain cover and clear any standing debris.
  2. Put a little water in the base of the shower — enough to cover the plunger cup.
  3. Seal the plunger over the drain and make sure there is water around the rim for a seal.
  4. Pump firmly 10–15 times, keeping the seal, finishing with a strong pull.
  5. If the shower has an overflow opening, block it with a wet cloth so pressure is directed at the clog.
  6. Run hot water afterward to check the flow.

Note: plunging is much less effective on a shower than on a sink, because the shower drain is open and wide. Treat it as a second option, not the first.

Step 4: Use a Handheld Auger Only if Needed

A small hand auger reaches further than a strip, but it carries risks in a bathroom.

  1. Use a small handheld auger, not a full-size machine snake.
  2. Feed the cable in gently and stop at resistance.
  3. Do not force the cable. Forcing it can scratch the fixture or, worse, damage the trap or the pipe joint.
  4. Turn the handle as you feed, which helps the head work past bends.
  5. Pull the cable out slowly, bringing debris with it.
  6. Never use a toilet auger or a machine snake in a shower drain — they can scratch and crack the fixture.

If the auger meets a hard blockage you cannot work past, stop. That is the point where the clog is beyond the trap and the job belongs to a plumber.

Step 5: Flush With Hot Water and Dish Soap

Once the mat is out, deal with the soap scum film that remains.

  1. Pour a kettle of hot — not boiling — water down the drain. Boiling water can soften or distort some plastic pipework, so hot tap-to-kettle temperature is the safe range.
  2. Add a squirt of dish soap and let it sit briefly, then flush again. Dish soap is a surfactant and helps lift the fatty residue that water alone will not move.
  3. Run the shower for a few minutes to carry everything through.
  4. Check the flow. If it is still slow, repeat from Step 2.

Do not rely on hot water alone as a fix. It moves soap scum temporarily and the residue re-solidifies further down the pipe, which moves the problem rather than solving it.

Step 6: Clear the Trap if Nothing Else Worked

  1. Put a bucket and towels under the trap — this is the messiest option.
  2. Unscrew the trap’s clean-out plug if it has one, which is often enough.
  3. If there is no plug, unscrew the trap assembly using a wrench, loosening the slip nuts by hand first.
  4. Clear it out and rinse it before refitting.
  5. Hand-tighten the slip nuts, then a small turn with a wrench. Overtightening cracks plastic traps.
  6. Run water and check for leaks before putting everything back.

Step 7: Prevent It Coming Back

This is where the real value is. A shower clog is highly predictable.

  1. Fit a hair catcher or strainer over or in the drain. This is the single most effective measure.
  2. Empty it every few days rather than waiting for it to fill — a full catcher stops draining and defeats the purpose.
  3. Once a month, run hot water and dish soap through the drain to keep the soap scum film from building.
  4. Use a barbed strip monthly as cheap maintenance if your household has long hair.
  5. Brush or comb hair before showering where practical, which removes what would otherwise shed in the drain.

Consider an enzyme-based maintainer for slow drains. These use bacteria or enzymes to break down organic matter over time. They are a maintenance product, not a fix for an existing clog, and they work slowly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the fastest way to unclog a shower drain?

Remove the cover and pull out any visible debris, then use a barbed plastic drain strip — push it in, pull it back slowly, and repeat until it comes out clean. It removes the hair mat in a few minutes without chemicals.

Does drain cleaner work on shower clogs?

Usually poorly. Shower clogs are hair bound with soap scum, and hair is protein — the products that break it down need long contact times and are hazardous. Worse, if it does not work you then reach into a pipe full of chemicals.

Why does my shower drain keep clogging?

Because hair and soap scum form a mat that rebuilds continuously. Fit a hair catcher over or in the drain and empty it every few days. That single measure prevents most recurrences.

Is it safe to use a drain snake in a shower?

A small handheld auger is, if used gently and only after a strip has failed. Never force the cable, and never use a toilet auger or a machine snake, which can scratch and crack the shower fixture.

How do I know if the clog is more than just the shower?

Run water in the other fixtures. If the sink, toilet, and tub are all slow, or if running the shower backs up another drain, the blockage is downstream in the main line and needs a plumber.

Can I pour boiling water down a shower drain?

Use hot rather than boiling water. Boiling water can soften or distort plastic pipework, which is common in modern waste lines. Hot water plus a squirt of dish soap works better anyway, because the soap lifts the fatty residue.

What is soap scum and why does it clog drains?

It is the insoluble residue formed when soap meets hard water — calcium and magnesium salts of fatty acids. It does not dissolve in water, so it builds up on the pipe wall and binds hair into a mat.

Should I remove the shower trap?

Only if the strip, plunger, and auger have all failed. Put a bucket underneath, use the clean-out plug if there is one, hand-tighten slip nuts and add only a small turn with a wrench, then check for leaks before refitting.

Do enzyme drain cleaners work?

They work slowly as a maintenance product, breaking down organic buildup over time. They are not a fix for an existing clog. Use them after the drain has been cleared mechanically.
