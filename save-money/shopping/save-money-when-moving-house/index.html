How to Save Money When Moving House

Save Money Shopping Published October 17, 2026 10 min read

Most moving advice focuses on the transaction: compare three quotes, pack your own boxes, rent a truck. All reasonable. But they all treat the volume of your belongings as a fixed input, and it is not. It is the variable that drives nearly every cost in a move.

The ranking of what actually moves the number:

LeverEffect on total costEffort
Reducing volumeLarge, and compounds across every other costHigh, but pays twice
Timing (day, month, season)Moderate to largeLow — just scheduling
Labour model (DIY vs full service)LargeHigh for DIY
Free or cheap packing materialsSmall to moderateLow
Insurance and add-onsSmall, but avoidableLow
Negotiating the quoteSmall to moderateLow

Volume is first because it is the only lever that multiplies. Fewer items means fewer boxes, less packing material, a smaller truck, fewer hours of labour, and less to unpack at the other end. It is the one decision that shows up in every line of the estimate.

1. Treat Volume as the Currency

Movers price by weight or by cubic volume plus labour hours. Both scale directly with how much you own.

Do the arithmetic before you decide what to keep. A useful mental model: moving an item costs roughly the same order of magnitude as its share of the total volume, and replacing it later often costs less than moving it — especially for heavy, low-value furniture.

Items that are usually worth leaving behind:

  • Heavy particleboard furniture that costs more to move than to replace
  • Half-used paint, solvents, and chemicals (many movers will not take them anyway)
  • Old mattresses and box springs past their useful life
  • Expired pantry goods and frozen food
  • Paper records you can digitise
  • Duplicates and the “someday” pile

A practical test: for anything bulky and low-value, look up its replacement price. If moving it costs a meaningful fraction of that, and you would not buy it again today, it should not travel.

Start early. Decluttering in the last three days before a move does not work, because decisions under time pressure default to “pack it.” Give yourself several weeks, and go room by room rather than item by item — the approach in decluttering without regret is built for exactly this situation.

2. Time the Move Around the Demand Curve

Moving prices follow demand, and demand is highly seasonal and highly predictable.

Peak:

  • End of the month (leases turn over)
  • Fridays, weekends, and the first and last days of the month
  • Summer, especially June through August
  • The last week of the month in summer is the absolute peak

Cheaper:

  • Mid-month
  • Weekdays, particularly Tuesday through Thursday
  • Late autumn through early spring
  • Mornings rather than afternoons, since a job that runs late costs more

If you have any scheduling freedom, shifting from the last Saturday of June to a Wednesday in October can change the price substantially for an identical job. Even within the same week, a mid-month weekday is usually cheaper than a month-end weekend.

Also consider school and work calendars. Peak moving season overlaps with school holidays largely because families move between school years, which is why the demand curve is so steep.

3. Get Quotes That Are Actually Comparable

A quote is only comparable if it is based on the same information.

Insist on the same basis for each:

  • Binding or not-to-exceed estimates rather than open hourly guesses where possible. A non-binding estimate can rise on the day, and it often does.
  • In-person or video survey, not a phone estimate from a room count. Volume is what they are pricing; make sure they have seen it.
  • The same inventory. If you tell one company about the piano and not another, the comparison is meaningless.
  • The same access conditions. Stairs, lift availability, distance from door to truck, parking restrictions, and narrow streets all add time.

Ask what is not included, because this is where estimates diverge:

  • Packing materials and packing labour
  • Disassembly and reassembly
  • Long carry or shuttle fees if a truck cannot park close
  • Stair or lift charges
  • Fuel surcharges and travel time
  • Piano, safe, or oversized item fees
  • Storage-in-transit, if you have a gap between homes
  • Valuation coverage beyond the basic minimum

Ask about the minimum. Many companies have a minimum hours charge, which means a very small move can cost more per item than you expect — another argument for reducing volume rather than “just moving a few things.”

4. Choose the Labour Model Honestly

The three models have different break-even points, and the right answer depends on distance and volume more than on bravado.

Full-service movers — they pack, load, drive, unload, and often unpack.

  • Best for: long distances, large homes, tight timelines, anything involving very heavy or valuable items
  • The premium is largely labour, and it buys speed and liability coverage
  • Worth it when the alternative is days of your own time plus a genuine injury risk

Truck or trailer rental — you drive.

  • Best for: moves where you can supply the labour, moderate distance, moderate volume
  • Costs scale with truck size and distance, plus fuel and insurance
  • The truck size estimate matters; going too small forces a second trip, which is the classic false economy

Container or portable storage — they drop a container, you load it, they move it.

  • Best for: flexible timing, situations with a gap between homes, and moves where you want to load at your own pace
  • Usually cheaper than full service and more expensive than pure DIY
  • The loading pace is yours, which reduces the time pressure that leads to poor packing

The honest self-assessment: the DIY saving is real, but it is wages for physically demanding work with a genuine injury risk. Factor in whether you actually have people who will help, whether you have the equipment (dolly, straps, blankets), and whether you can drive a large vehicle. A back injury is not a saving.

5. Get Packing Materials for Free or Cheap

Boxes are the most visible moving cost and the easiest to eliminate.

Where to get them free:

  • Liquor stores — boxes are sturdy, uniform, and often have dividers, which is ideal for glassware
  • Grocery stores — produce boxes; banana boxes are famously strong and stackable
  • Bookshops and pharmacies
  • Workplace or office recycling, if permitted
  • Local community groups and marketplace listings, often free the day after someone moves

Where to be careful:

  • Used boxes from unknown sources can carry pests. Inspect for signs before use, especially if you have had pantry pest issues.
  • Wardrobe boxes are worth renting or buying if you have hanging clothes, but they are bulky; consider bundling hanging clothes in large bags instead.
  • Mattress and furniture covers are cheap and genuinely protect value.
  • Bubble wrap is over-used. Towels, bedding, and clothing cushion well and you have to move them anyway.

Buy tape properly. Cheap tape that fails mid-move costs more than the tape. A dispenser gun is worth having.

6. Avoid the Costs That Hide

The moving quote is not the whole cost.

  • Cleaning the old place — if a deposit or sale condition depends on it, budget time or money for it explicitly.
  • Overlap rent or mortgage — a week of double housing can exceed every other saving. Negotiate a slight overlap rather than a same-day handover if you can, but be deliberate about it.
  • Utility transfer and reconnection fees, deposits, and setup charges.
  • Address change and forwarding, plus updating everything that bills you.
  • Takeaway meals during the days your kitchen is unavailable — a real and commonly underestimated cost. Pack a box with a kettle, mugs, a pan, and basic utensils so you can feed yourself on day one.
  • Time off work, which is often the largest true cost of a DIY move.
  • Storage, if you cannot move everything at once. Storage fees have a way of becoming permanent; set an end date before you start.

7. Pack in a Way That Saves Money at the Other End

Poor packing costs money twice — in damaged goods and in unpacking time.

  • Label by room and by priority, not just by contents. “Kitchen — open first” is more useful than “kitchen stuff.”
  • Pack an essentials box that travels with you: documents, medications, chargers, basic tools, toilet paper, a change of clothes, and the kettle.
  • Do not overfill boxes. Heavy boxes break, and broken boxes cost time and sometimes contents.
  • Disassemble what you can and tape hardware to the item in a labelled bag.
  • Photograph cable setups before disconnecting electronics.
  • Weigh the value of reassembly time against the item itself — another reason the decluttering pass matters.

Why This Works

The cost structure of a move makes volume the dominant variable. Every other line item — boxes, packing material, truck size, labour hours, and insurance valuation — is a function of how much you are moving. Reduce the input and every output falls together, which is unusual among cost-saving strategies where levers are normally independent.

Timing works because moving capacity is perishable. A moving company’s trucks and crews either work on a given day or they do not, and demand is concentrated at month-ends, weekends, and summer. Pricing follows that curve, so the same job has genuinely different prices on different days. Scheduling is the cheapest lever available because it costs nothing but calendar awareness.

Comparable quotes matter because the industry quotes on estimates that are frequently revised. A non-binding estimate is not a price; it is a guess with a direction. Insisting on a survey and a binding or not-to-exceed figure converts a guess into a number you can plan around.

The labour decision is really a question about what your time and risk are worth, and it is worth answering honestly rather than defaulting. The DIY saving is real but it is paid for in physical work with injury risk, and the crossover point depends on volume and distance rather than on a rule of thumb.

Finally, the hidden costs are where budgets actually break. Takeaway meals, overlapping housing, and storage that never ends are not exotic — they are the normal failure modes, and naming them in advance is most of the defence.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the single biggest way to save money on a move?

Reduce what you move. Volume drives boxes, materials, truck size, labour hours, and insurance valuation, so it is the only lever that reduces every line at once.

Is it cheaper to move mid-month?

Usually yes. Demand concentrates at month-end when leases turn over, so a mid-month weekday is generally cheaper for the same job.

Should I hire movers or do it myself?

It depends on volume, distance, and whether you genuinely have help. Full service buys speed, liability coverage, and removes a real injury risk. Pure DIY is cheapest in cash and most expensive in time and risk.

How far in advance should I book?

Several weeks for peak season, and earlier if your dates are fixed. Off-peak and mid-month dates have more availability, which is part of why they are cheaper.

Where can I get free moving boxes?

Liquor stores for sturdy uniform boxes with dividers, grocery stores for produce boxes, and local marketplace listings after someone else moves. Inspect used boxes before use.

Are binding estimates worth asking for?

Yes. A binding or not-to-exceed estimate turns a guess into a number. Non-binding estimates can and often do increase on moving day.

What costs do people forget when budgeting a move?

Overlapping rent or mortgage, cleaning the old place, utility deposits and setup fees, takeaway meals, time off work, and storage. These are the usual reasons a move exceeds its budget.

Is portable storage cheaper than full-service movers?

Usually yes, and more expensive than a pure truck rental. It is a good fit when you want to load at your own pace or have a gap between homes.

Should I move my plants and pantry food?

Check restrictions — many movers will not take plants across long distances or perishables at all. Plan to give away or use up food, and check rules for plants in advance.

How do I avoid paying for a second trip?

Get an accurate volume estimate, ideally from an in-person or video survey, and size the vehicle with margin. A second trip costs a full extra cycle of loading, driving, and unloading.
