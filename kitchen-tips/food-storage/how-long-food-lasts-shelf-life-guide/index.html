How Long Food Actually Lasts: The Shelf Life Guide

Kitchen Tips Food Storage Published October 14, 2026 8 min read

Expiration dates are the biggest source of unnecessary food waste in a home kitchen. They are also widely misunderstood.

What a date label actually means:

  • “Best by” / “Best if used by” — a quality date, not a safety date. The food may taste slightly different after this date but is not unsafe.
  • “Sell by” — a date for the retailer to rotate stock. It has almost nothing to do with how long the food is good at home.
  • “Use by” — the closest thing to a safety date, and the only one worth taking seriously. It appears mainly on infant formula and some ready-to-eat items.
  • “Freeze by” — a quality date for meat and fish, indicating when to freeze for best results.

Except for infant formula, no federal law in the United States requires these dates. They are voluntary estimates. That means the date on your yogurt is the manufacturer’s guess, not a threshold at which the food becomes dangerous.

The numbers below are the practical windows — how long a reasonably handled product actually stays good at home.

Step 1: Understand the three variables that change every number

Every shelf life figure is an estimate based on three conditions. Change any of them and the window moves.

Temperature. This is the dominant variable. A refrigerator at 37°F (3°C) keeps food far longer than one at 45°F (7°C). Lower your fridge temperature and every number in this article improves.

Handling. How long the food sat out, whether it was touched with dirty hands or utensils, and whether it was cooled quickly all matter. A container of soup left on the counter for three hours before refrigerating loses days.

Oxygen and moisture. Exposure to air causes oxidation and molds; trapped moisture grows bacteria. Sealing and drying buy time.

So treat the tables as starting points for your own kitchen, not guarantees.

Fatty foods lose quality fastest in the freezer. Salmon, mackerel, and ice cream all have short windows because fat oxidizes and turns rancid even when frozen.

A note on refreezing: meat thawed in the refrigerator may be refrozen if it was thawed safely. Meat thawed on a counter or in warm water should be cooked, not refrozen.

Three pantry rules worth knowing:

  • Honey and salt do not spoil. Crystallized honey is fine — warm the jar. Clumped salt just needs a grain of rice or a quick stir.
  • Whole wheat flour goes rancid within months. The germ oil oxidizes. Store it in the fridge or freezer, not the pantry.
  • Ground spices fade long before they spoil. They are not unsafe, just flavorless. Smell them: if a spice has no aroma when crushed, replace it.

Step 5: How to date your own food

Manufacturer dates tell you something, but your own date tells you more. Two habits turn this knowledge into practice:

  1. Write the open date on the container. Not the purchase date — the date you opened it. Two seconds with tape and a marker, and now every “how long has this been in here” question is answered.
  2. Write the freeze date on freezer bags. Frozen food becomes unidentifiable within a month or two. Contents plus date is the minimum.

Then keep a printed copy of these tables taped inside a cabinet door. The reference is only useful if it is reachable while you are holding the container.

Why This Works

Food safety and food quality follow different rules, and confusing them is what causes both waste and illness.

Safety depends on pathogen growth: temperature, time, and moisture. That is why the safety windows are short for wet, protein-rich foods and why the two-hour counter rule matters.

Quality depends on oxidation, moisture loss, and enzyme activity. That is why the freezer table has short windows for fatty foods and why spices fade — none of which makes food dangerous.

Two clear signals separate the two: the smell test (spoilage bacteria produce obvious off odors, and you should trust that) and the mold rule (mold on a hard, firm food can be cut away with a wide margin; mold on soft or liquid food means the whole thing is contaminated and must be discarded). Knowing which variable you are dealing with lets you stop throwing away food that is merely past its peak.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is food safe to eat after the expiration date?

Usually yes, for anything with a "best by" or "sell by" label. Those are quality dates. Only "use by" dates function as safety dates, and they appear mainly on infant formula and a few ready-to-eat items. Judge by storage conditions and smell for everything else.

How long can leftovers stay in the fridge?

Three to four days for most cooked foods. Cooked rice is the exception at one day, because of a specific bacterium that survives reheating. Always cool food quickly and refrigerate within two hours of cooking.

Does meat go bad in the freezer?

No — freezing stops pathogen growth indefinitely, so it never becomes unsafe from time alone. But quality declines, especially for fatty fish and ground meat. Freezer-burned food is safe but dry and bland.

Can I eat food with mold on it?

If it is a hard, firm food — hard cheese, firm vegetables, salami — cut off at least one inch around the mold and use the rest. If it is soft or liquid — bread, yogurt, jam, soft cheese, leftovers — discard the whole thing. Mold roots extend through soft foods.

Why does milk last longer on a middle shelf than in the door?

The door is the warmest and most temperature-variable spot in the refrigerator because it opens to room air constantly. Milk stored in the door sours days earlier than the same milk on a middle shelf.

How do I know if eggs are still good?

Place an egg in a bowl of water. Fresh eggs sink and lie flat; older eggs tilt or stand up; a floating egg is old enough to discard. Eggs also keep far longer than most people think — three to five weeks from purchase in the fridge.

Does honey really never spoil?

Correct. Honey is acidic and low in water activity, so bacteria and mold cannot grow in it. Crystallization is a texture change, not spoilage — warm the jar gently to liquefy it.

Should I store flour in the freezer?

White flour is fine in the pantry for up to a year if sealed. Whole wheat flour should go in the fridge or freezer because the germ oil goes rancid within a few months. Freezing also protects flour from pantry moths.
