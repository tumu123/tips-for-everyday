How to Remove Mildew Smell from Laundry

Home Hacks Laundry Published October 14, 2026 7 min read
Advertisement

Musty laundry is not a detergent problem, and adding more detergent makes it worse. The smell is produced by bacteria and mold living in the fabric or in the washing machine itself, and the fix is to kill the organisms and remove their food source.

The counterintuitive part: over-dosing detergent is a leading cause. Excess detergent does not rinse out completely, leaves a residue in the fabric and in the machine, and that residue feeds the very microbes causing the smell. More soap leads to more smell.

Step 0: Find the source before treating the clothes

The smell is coming from one of three places. Diagnosing correctly saves repeating the wash.

SourceHow to identify itFix
The washing machineThe drum or gasket smells even when empty; every load comes out mustyClean the machine (Step 1)
The fabricOnly some items smell; others from the same load are fineTreat the fabric (Step 2)
The drying processItems smell fine wet but musty once driedFix drying time or airflow (Step 3)
StorageClothes smell after sitting in a drawer or closetFix storage humidity and airflow (Step 4)

The empty-machine test: run a rinse cycle with no clothes and no detergent. Open the door and smell the drum. If it smells musty, the machine is the source and you must clean it first — otherwise every future load will smell.

Step 1: Clean the washing machine (do this first)

If the machine is the source, no amount of treating clothes will help.

Front-loaders

Front-loaders are the most common culprits because a rubber door gasket traps water and residue.

  1. Wipe the door gasket with a cloth dipped in white vinegar. Pull back the folds — that is where the black mold grows and where most people never look.
  2. Remove and clean the detergent drawer. Soak it in warm water with vinegar.
  3. Clean the drain pump filter if your machine has one. It collects lint, coins, and organic matter that putrefies.
  4. Run a hot empty cycle with 2 cups of white vinegar in the drum, or a dedicated washing machine cleaner.
  5. Follow with a second hot cycle with 1/2 cup baking soda if the smell persists, or use oxygen bleach for a stronger treatment.
  6. Leave the door and the detergent drawer open after every use so the interior dries. This single habit prevents most recurrence.

Top-loaders

  1. Fill the drum with hot water on the largest setting.
  2. Add 2 cups white vinegar and run a full cycle.
  3. Run a second cycle with 1/2 cup baking soda if needed.
  4. Wipe the top rim and the fabric softener dispenser, both of which collect residue.

Never mix vinegar and chlorine bleach. Bleach plus acid produces chlorine gas. Use one or the other, with a rinse in between.

Note on fabric softener and scent beads: these coat the drum and the fabric with waxy residue that traps soil and microbes. Many people find that stopping their use eliminates a chronic musty smell.

Step 2: Treat the smelly fabric

Once the machine is clean, treat the clothing itself.

The vinegar soak (for most fabrics)

  1. Fill a tub or the machine with cool water.
  2. Add 1 cup white vinegar per gallon (or 2 cups for a full machine load).
  3. Submerge and soak 30–60 minutes.
  4. Run a normal wash cycle with a normal amount of detergent — not more.
  5. Check the smell before drying. If it persists, repeat.

Vinegar works because it is acidic. Mildew and the bacteria causing odor prefer a neutral to slightly alkaline environment. Acidifying the fabric makes it inhospitable, and vinegar also helps dissolve the detergent residue that feeds the microbes.

The oxygen bleach soak (for white and colorfast cotton)

  1. Dissolve oxygen bleach in warm water per the package.
  2. Submerge and soak 1–2 hours, or overnight for severe cases.
  3. Wash normally.

Oxygen bleach is more effective against mold spores than vinegar alone, and it does not damage colorfast cotton. Do not use it on wool, silk, or leather.

For heavy mold contamination

If the fabric shows visible black spots or the smell is severe, vinegar alone may not be enough. Use oxygen bleach, and if the fabric is a towel or something non-delicate, consider a hot wash with oxygen bleach. Items with deep mold growth may need to be discarded, especially anything that contacts the face or a wound.

Step 3: Fix the drying

Wet fabric left too long is where the smell starts. Drying is not just about removing water — it is about removing it fast enough.

  • Do not leave wet laundry in the machine. Even a few hours at room temperature lets bacteria multiply. Move it to the dryer or the line promptly.
  • Do not overload the dryer. A packed dryer traps moisture and extends drying time, which allows odor to develop.
  • Ensure adequate airflow. Clean the lint filter before every load. A clogged lint filter both extends drying time and is a fire hazard.
  • Check the dryer vent periodically. A blocked vent traps moist air in the drum.
  • Dry thoroughly. Fabric that is dried to 95 percent and then folded will develop a musty smell. Dry completely.
  • Line drying is superb for odor if you have the space and the weather. Sunlight also has a mild disinfecting effect.
  • If the weather is humid, line-drying indoors can make the smell worse. Use the dryer or a dehumidifier in that case.

Step 4: Fix the storage so it does not come back

Clean laundry can develop the smell again in storage if the conditions are wrong.

  • Store only fully dry items. Convinced something is dry? It usually is not. When in doubt, another ten minutes.
  • Do not pack a drawer tightly. Overstuffed drawers trap humid air and no airflow reaches the fabric.
  • Avoid plastic bins for long-term storage. Plastic traps moisture. Use breathable cotton bags or leave the bin slightly vented.
  • Keep the closet dry. A dehumidifier in a humid climate does more for laundry freshness than any product.
  • Do not store laundry in a basement or damp garage without moisture control.

For smoke and cigarette odors, vinegar is the most effective household treatment, followed by oxygen bleach for whites and colorfast items. Do not put smoke-saturated items in the dryer before treatment — heat sets the odor into the fabric.

For gasoline or solvent smells, safety comes first. Wash separately and air dry in a well-ventilated area, away from any ignition source. Do not put solvent-soaked fabric directly into a dryer.

Why This Works

The musty smell comes from microbial volatile compounds — the byproducts of bacteria and mold metabolizing organic material. Two conditions are required for this to happen: moisture and a food source.

Moisture is addressed by fixing the wet window: moving laundry promptly, drying fully, and letting the machine itself dry between uses. A damp drum is a permanent microbial habitat, which is why cleaning the machine comes before treating the clothes.

The food source is detergent residue, body oils, and lint. This is why using more detergent backfires: the excess does not rinse out, it stays in the fabric and the machine, and it feeds the problem. Reducing detergent and removing fabric softener removes the food.

Vinegar works by acidification. Odor-causing bacteria and mold prefer neutral to slightly alkaline conditions, and vinegar’s acidity makes the environment hostile while also dissolving the alkaline residue that detergent leaves behind.

Oxygen bleach works by oxidation, attacking the microbial cell structure and breaking the chemical bonds that produce odor compounds. It is more powerful than vinegar against established mold, and it is safe for colorfast cotton — unlike chlorine bleach, which damages wool, silk, and spandex.

Sunlight contributes UV exposure, which has a mild sanitizing effect on the fabric surface — one reason line-dried laundry smells fresh when the process is done right.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do my clothes smell musty even after washing? Usually because the washing machine itself is the source. Run a rinse cycle with no clothes and smell the drum. If it smells musty, clean the machine before treating the clothes, or every future load will be recontaminated.

Does vinegar kill mold in laundry? Vinegar’s acidity makes the environment hostile to mold and bacteria and helps dissolve the detergent residue that feeds them. It is effective for mild cases. For established mold or a severe smell, use oxygen bleach, which is more powerful.

How much vinegar should I use in a laundry soak? About 1 cup of white vinegar per gallon of water for a tub soak, or 2 cups for a full machine load. Soak 30 to 60 minutes, then wash normally.

Can I use bleach to remove the smell? Chlorine bleach kills microbes but damages wool, silk, and spandex, and yellows synthetic blends. Oxygen bleach is the safer whitening and deodorizing agent for most fabrics.

Why is my front-loader worse than a top-loader for smells? Front-loaders use less water, so residue is not rinsed away as thoroughly, and their rubber door gaskets trap water and lint in the folds. That combination makes them more prone to odor. Wiping the gasket and leaving the door open after use largely solves it.

Does putting clothes in the freezer remove the smell? It can reduce odor temporarily but does not kill the bacteria and mold responsible. It is not a solution, and a cold item brought into a warm room can promote condensation.

How do I remove cigarette smoke smell from clothes? Soak in a vinegar solution, then wash with oxygen bleach for whites and colorfast items. Do not put untreated smoky items in the dryer, because heat sets the odor. Several treatments may be needed for heavy smoke exposure.

How do I stop the smell coming back? Leave the machine door open after each use, wipe the gasket weekly on front-loaders, use less detergent, skip fabric softener and scent beads, move laundry promptly, dry completely, and run a monthly cleaning cycle.
