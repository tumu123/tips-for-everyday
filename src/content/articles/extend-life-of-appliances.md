Major appliances fail for predictable reasons, and most of those reasons are maintenance issues rather than manufacturing defects. A refrigerator with dusty coils runs hotter and dies sooner. A washing machine with a clogged filter leaks. A dryer with a blocked vent is a fire risk that also shortens motor life.

**The economic case:** A mid-range refrigerator costs roughly $1,200–2,500, a washer $700–1,400, a dryer $600–1,200. Extending each by three to five years defers thousands in replacement costs. The maintenance that achieves this requires a few hours per year.

**The schedule below is organized by frequency**, so you can batch tasks — most of the monthly work takes under 30 minutes total.

## The Complete Maintenance Schedule

| Frequency | Tasks | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly | Vacuum refrigerator coils, clean range hood filter, inspect washer hoses, clean dryer lint trap housing | 30 min |
| Quarterly | Clean dishwasher filter and seals, clean washing machine, vacuum dryer vent, check ice maker line | 45 min |
| Semi-annually | Clean oven, inspect and level appliances, test GFCI outlets, replace water filters | 60 min |
| Annually | Replace washer hoses if rubber, deep clean refrigerator gaskets, service HVAC, inspect dryer vent duct | 90 min |
| As needed | Clean dryer vent exterior, replace refrigerator water filter | varies |

## Category 1: Refrigeration

### Monthly: Vacuum the condenser coils

This is the highest-value appliance maintenance task in the home.

1. **Unplug the refrigerator** or turn off its breaker.
2. **Pull it away from the wall**, leaving enough room to work behind it.
3. **Locate the coils** — either at the back (older models) or behind a grille at the bottom front (most modern models).
4. **Vacuum with a brush attachment.** Work gently along the coil fins. A coil brush helps but is optional.
5. **Vacuum or sweep the floor** behind and under the refrigerator while you have access.
6. **Push it back**, leaving a 1–2 inch gap from the wall for airflow.
7. **Restore power.**

**Why it matters:** Dusty coils cannot dissipate heat, so the compressor runs longer and hotter. This increases energy use measurably and shortens compressor life — the most expensive component to replace.

### Quarterly: Check the door gaskets

1. **Close the door on a dollar bill** so it is half in, half out.
2. **Pull the bill out.** If it slides freely, the gasket is not sealing.
3. **Clean the gasket** with warm soapy water — dirt prevents a proper seal.
4. **Check for cracks, tears, or deformation.** A damaged gasket should be replaced; it is an inexpensive part.
5. **Test the door alignment.** A door that sags or does not close flush needs hinge adjustment.

### Semi-annually: Clean the drain pan and check the defrost

1. **Locate the drain pan** (usually behind the front grille at the bottom).
2. **Remove and wash it** — it collects condensation and can grow mold.
3. **Check the drain tube** for clogs. A clogged drain causes water to pool inside or under the refrigerator.
4. **Listen for the defrost cycle** or check whether frost is building up in the freezer. Excessive frost indicates a defrost system problem that should be serviced before it damages the evaporator.

### Annually: Deep clean and inspect

1. **Empty and clean the interior** with a solution of baking soda and water.
2. **Vacuum the door gasket folds** with a brush attachment.
3. **Check the seal around the whole door** with the dollar bill test at multiple points.
4. **Replace the water filter** if the refrigerator has a dispenser (typically every 6 months, but replace when the flow slows or the taste changes).
5. **Verify the temperature** with an appliance thermometer: 37°F (3°C) for the fridge, 0°F (−18°C) for the freezer.

## Category 2: Laundry

### Monthly: Inspect hoses and lint trap housing

**Washing machine hoses:**

1. **Check both hot and cold supply hoses** for bulges, cracks, stiffness, or moisture.
2. **Feel along the length of each hose** for soft spots or blistering.
3. **Check the connections** at both the machine and the wall for drips.
4. **Replace any hose showing any of these signs immediately.** A burst hose causes thousands of dollars in water damage and is a leading cause of home insurance claims.

**Dryer lint trap housing:**

1. **Remove the lint trap** as usual and clean it.
2. **Look into the empty slot** with a flashlight. Lint accumulates beyond the screen where the trap sits.
3. **Vacuum out the housing** with a crevice attachment.
4. **Check the flexible duct** behind the dryer for kinks or crushing.

### Quarterly: Clean the washing machine and dryer vent

**Washing machine (see our full guide for details):**

1. Run a hot empty cycle with 2 cups of white vinegar.
2. Clean the door gasket (front-loaders) — this is the main source of odor.
3. Clean the detergent drawer.
4. Clean the drain filter (front-loaders) — expect some water spillage.

**Dryer vent line:**

1. **Unplug the dryer** (or turn off the breaker for gas dryers).
2. **Disconnect the duct** from the back of the dryer.
3. **Vacuum inside the dryer's exhaust port** and as far into the duct as you can reach.
4. **Inspect the duct** for lint accumulation. If a substantial amount has built up, the entire duct may need cleaning or replacement.
5. **Reconnect the duct** and ensure it is not crushed behind the machine.
6. **Check the exterior vent flap** — it should open freely when the dryer runs.

**Why it matters:** A blocked dryer vent is a leading cause of house fires and forces the dryer to run longer, which wears out the motor and heating element prematurely.

### Annually: Replace rubber hoses

1. **Replace standard rubber washing machine hoses every 5 years**, or immediately if any sign of wear appears.
2. **Consider upgrading to braided stainless steel hoses.** They cost more but resist bursting far better and last longer.
3. **Most manufacturers recommend replacing the drain hose** if it shows cracks or kinks.

## Category 3: Dishwasher

### Monthly: Clean the filter and check for leaks

1. **Remove the filter** at the bottom of the tub (most modern dishwashers have a removable filter).
2. **Rinse it under running water** and scrub with a soft brush.
3. **Check the drain area** for debris.
4. **Look under the dishwasher** for signs of leaks — dark stains or warped flooring.
5. **Check the door gasket** for cracks or debris.

### Quarterly: Deep clean

1. **Run a hot cycle with a cup of white vinegar** in a dishwasher-safe bowl on the top rack.
2. **Wipe the door gasket** with a damp cloth.
3. **Clean around the door hinges** where food debris collects.
4. **Check the spray arms** for clogged holes. Remove them if possible and clear with a toothpick.
5. **Clean the exterior panel** matching the material (stainless steel, painted, or panel-ready).

### Annually

1. **Check the water supply line** for leaks at both ends.
2. **Inspect the drain hose** under the sink for kinks.
3. **Test for proper draining** — water should be fully gone at the end of the cycle.
4. **Run a cycle with a dishwasher cleaner** if mineral buildup is visible.

A clogged filter is the usual reason glasses come out gritty — [cleaning the dishwasher filter](/home-hacks/appliances/clean-dishwasher-filter/) covers the monthly routine.

## Category 4: Oven and Range

### Quarterly: Clean thoroughly

1. **For self-cleaning ovens:** Run the self-clean cycle per the manual. Ventilate well — this cycle produces smoke and odor.
2. **For non-self-cleaning ovens:** Use an oven cleaner following label directions, or a baking soda paste for lighter soil.
3. **Clean the oven door glass** inside and out.
4. **Clean the racks** — soak in hot soapy water, then scrub.
5. **Clean the stovetop** and burner grates.
6. **Check the door seal** for damage. A worn seal wastes significant energy.

**Note on self-cleaning cycles:** The high heat of a self-clean cycle can damage the control board or heating element on some ovens, particularly older models. If your manual warns against frequent use, clean manually instead.

### Annually

1. **Check the level** of the range with a spirit level. Uneven ranges bake unevenly.
2. **Inspect the gas connections** if it is a gas range — look for a smell or visible damage. If you detect a gas smell, leave the house and call the utility.
3. **Clean the range hood filter** (see our dedicated guide).
4. **Check the oven temperature accuracy** with an oven thermometer.

## Category 5: Water Heater

### Annually: Flush the tank (tank-style heaters)

Sediment accumulates at the bottom of tank water heaters and reduces efficiency while shortening tank life.

1. **Turn off the power** (electric) or set the gas valve to pilot (gas).
2. **Turn off the cold water supply** to the heater.
3. **Connect a garden hose** to the drain valve at the bottom and run it to a floor drain, bucket, or outside.
4. **Open the drain valve** and let the tank drain.
5. **Open a hot water faucet** elsewhere in the house to allow air in and improve flow.
6. **Briefly open the cold water supply** to stir up remaining sediment, then drain again.
7. **Close the drain valve**, remove the hose, and refill the tank completely **before restoring power**.
8. **Restore power.**

**Safety note:** Never restore power to an electric water heater before the tank is full — the heating element will burn out immediately.

**Also check:** the temperature-pressure relief valve (test it annually), the anode rod (should be replaced every 3–5 years for tank longevity), and for any signs of corrosion or leaking at the fittings.

**Tankless water heaters:** Do not use the flush method above without a pump kit and manufacturer-specific instructions. Descaling a tankless heater requires circulating a vinegar solution through the unit.

## Why This Works

| Failure mode | Prevented by | Why it happens |
|---|---|---|
| Compressor failure | Clean condenser coils | Dust forces the compressor to run hotter and longer |
| Refrigerator inefficiency | Seal maintenance | Leaky gaskets make the compressor work continuously |
| Washer odor and water damage | Gasket and hose care | Bacteria grow in trapped moisture; old hoses burst |
| Dryer fire and motor failure | Vent cleaning | Lint blocks airflow, overheats the element, and is flammable |
| Dishwasher leaks and odors | Filter and seal cleaning | Food debris clogs the filter and degrades seals |
| Water heater failure | Annual flushing | Sediment insulates the burner and corrodes the tank |
| Reduced cleaning performance | Filter and spray arm care | Clogged components cannot do their job |

The underlying pattern: **most appliance failures are thermal or moisture problems.** Heat cannot escape (dust, lint, sediment), or moisture accumulates where it should not (gaskets, hoses, seals). Almost all maintenance addresses one of those two.

Keeping a machine alive is one way to avoid the purchase; buying the replacement at half price is the other — [what a refurbished warranty covers](/save-money/shopping/buying-refurbished-appliances/) covers what the warranty on that unit actually covers.

## What NOT to Do

| Mistake | Why it's a problem |
|---|---|
| Powering on an electric water heater before refilling | Burns out the heating element immediately |
| Ignoring a bulging or stiff washing machine hose | Burst hoses cause severe water damage and are a leading insurance claim |
| Never cleaning the dryer vent | Fire risk, and it shortens motor and element life |
| Using a knife or metal object inside a toaster | Risk of electric shock and damage to the heating elements |
| Running a self-clean cycle on an older oven frequently | Can damage the control board or element |
| Cleaning condenser coils with a wet cloth | Moisture plus dust creates a paste that is harder to remove and risks electrical components |
| Using regular dish soap in a dishwasher | Excessive suds can overflow and damage the machine |
| Blocking the refrigerator's airflow gap | Prevents heat dissipation and defeats the purpose of cleaning coils |
| Deferring small repairs | Small leaks become large ones, and small noises become failures |

## FAQ

**How often should I clean my refrigerator coils?**
Every six to twelve months for most homes. Every three months if you have pets, because pet hair accumulates quickly on the coils. This is the single highest-value appliance maintenance task.

**How long should appliances last?**
Typical lifespans with proper maintenance: refrigerator 12–17 years, washing machine 10–14, dryer 10–15, dishwasher 9–12, oven 13–15, water heater 8–12. Without maintenance, each of these drops by several years.

**What is the most common cause of appliance failure?**
Neglected maintenance rather than manufacturing defects. For refrigerators it is dust on the coils; for dryers it is lint in the vent; for water heaters it is sediment in the tank. All three are preventable with minimal effort.

**Should I replace washing machine hoses?**
Yes. Rubber hoses should be replaced every five years regardless of appearance, and immediately if they show any bulging, cracking, or stiffness. Braided stainless steel hoses last longer and resist bursting far better. Burst hoses cause severe water damage.

**How do I know if my dryer vent is clogged?**
Clothes take longer than one cycle to dry, the dryer feels hot to the touch on the outside, the exterior vent flap does not open fully, or you notice lint accumulating around the dryer. Any of these indicates restricted airflow.

**Do self-cleaning ovens damage the appliance?**
They can. The high heat of a self-clean cycle occasionally damages control boards, door gaskets, or heating elements, especially on older ovens. If your manual warns against frequent use, clean manually with an oven cleaner instead.

**How often should I flush a water heater?**
Annually for tank-style heaters, especially in areas with hard water. Sediment accumulates at the bottom of the tank, reducing efficiency and corroding the tank from the inside. Tankless heaters need periodic descaling instead, using a pump kit.

**Is an appliance warranty worth it?**
It depends. Extended warranties on major appliances typically cost 10–20% of the purchase price and frequently are not worth it for reliable brands with good maintenance. They are more often worth considering for appliances with high repair costs and known reliability issues. Maintaining the appliance properly is a better investment than most warranties.
