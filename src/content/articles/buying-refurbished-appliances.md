The word "refurbished" is not a standard, a grade, or a legal term. It is a marketing word, and it is applied to at least four genuinely different products: a machine the manufacturer rebuilt and re-certified, a machine a retailer took back and wiped down, a machine a third party repaired with unknown parts, and a machine that was returned, found to work, and relabelled.

Those four have wildly different risk profiles, and the warranty — not the price, not the condition — is what tells you which one you are looking at. A 90-day parts-and-labor warranty on a $900 refrigerator is not the same product as a one-year manufacturer warranty, even though both are described as refurbished.

## The Four Categories

**1. Manufacturer refurbished (factory recertified, factory reconditioned).** The unit goes back to the manufacturer or an authorised service centre, is inspected against a checklist, has failed components replaced with genuine parts, is cleaned, and is resold with a manufacturer warranty — typically 90 days to one year, sometimes with the remainder of the original warranty.

This is the best category. The parts are correct, the diagnostics were done by people with the service manuals, and the warranty is backed by a company that will still exist in a year. Prices are typically 30% to 50% below new.

**2. Retailer refurbished, open box, and scratch-and-dent.** A unit returned to a store, inspected (or not), and resold. Cosmetic damage is common and usually disclosed. The warranty is often the manufacturer's standard warranty — this is the key advantage of open box over third-party refurb — sometimes shortened, sometimes full. Scratch-and-dent units are frequently new units damaged in delivery, which means the mechanicals are untouched and only the side panel is dented.

This is the best value category when the warranty is the full manufacturer term and the damage is on a side that faces a wall.

**3. Third-party refurbisher.** A repair business that buys broken or returned units, fixes what is needed, and resells. Quality ranges from excellent to dangerous, and there is no way to tell from the listing. The warranty is the refurbisher's own, usually 30 to 90 days, and its value depends entirely on whether the business is still there when the compressor fails.

**4. Returns sold as-is.** A returned unit sold with no warranty or a short store return window. It saved the retailer the cost of processing a return, and the price reflects that. The risk is yours entirely.

If a listing does not say which of these it is, that is the answer: assume the worst of the four.

## What the Warranty Actually Covers

Read the warranty document, not the badge on the showroom floor. The differences that matter:

**Length.** New appliances typically carry one year parts and labour, with longer coverage on the sealed system (compressor, condenser, evaporator) — often five to ten years on the sealed system, sometimes parts only after the first year. Refurbished units commonly get 90 days, sometimes a year. Ask specifically whether the sealed-system coverage survives refurbishment; it usually does not.

**Parts and labour, or parts only.** A parts-only warranty on a refrigerator means a free $400 compressor and a $500 labour bill. This distinction is worth more than the length of the warranty.

**In-home or carry-in.** A carry-in warranty on a 300-pound refrigerator means you transport it to a service centre. On a microwave, carry-in is fine. On anything plumbed or over 100 pounds, in-home service is the only useful kind.

**Cosmetic exclusions.** Nearly universal. Dents, scratches, and finish defects are excluded even when they were disclosed at purchase. This is normal and not a red flag.

**Whether it is transferable** and whether an extended protection plan can be added. Some refurbished units cannot be covered by third-party extended warranties, which removes a fallback option.

**Who honours it.** A manufacturer warranty is honoured by the manufacturer's service network. A refurbisher's warranty is honoured by that business, if it answers the phone.

## The Discount That Makes It Worth It

Refurbished pricing only makes sense at a real discount, because you are buying a shorter warranty and an unknown history. A useful rule:

- **40% to 60% off new:** worth serious consideration, especially for manufacturer-refurbished units.
- **20% to 35% off:** marginal, unless the warranty is the full manufacturer term and the unit is open box rather than repaired.
- **Under 20% off:** buy new. The warranty alone is worth more than the difference.

Do not compare against the manufacturer's suggested price or the sticker on the showroom model. Compare against the price you can actually buy a new equivalent for this week, including delivery and installation.

## Which Appliances Are Good Refurbished Candidates, and Which Are Not

The general principle: **the simpler the machine, the better the refurbished bet.**

**Good candidates:**

- **Electric or gas ranges with no electronics.** Elements, burners, and thermostats are cheap and simple. A range with a failed board is a different story.
- **Top-load washing machines with mechanical controls.** Fewer boards, cheaper parts, easier diagnosis.
- **Dishwashers.** Usually a pump or a valve, and those are replaceable.
- **Microwaves.** Cheap enough that the downside is small, though a magnetron failure is uneconomic to repair.
- **Range hoods, disposals, and small appliances** with no electronics.

The general case for keeping a simple machine running is in [extending appliance life](/save-money/shopping/extend-life-of-appliances/); the case against is usually a control board that costs more than the machine is worth.

**Poor candidates:**

- **Refrigerators with sealed-system failures.** A compressor or evaporator replacement costs $600 to $1,000 and is often uneconomic on a discounted unit. If the refurbisher replaced the compressor, ask who did it and what warranty covers it.
- **Front-load washers and heat-pump dryers.** Control boards, inverter drives, and door-lock assemblies are $300 to $600 each, and they are where these machines fail.
- **Anything with a touchscreen or connected features.** The board is the machine.
- **Units that were flooded or stored damp.** Water damage shows up as corrosion on the frame, musty smell, and failing electronics six months later.
- **Very old units sold as refurbished.** A 15-year-old refrigerator costs $80 to $120 a year more to run than a new one. That is the entire saving, gone.

## Check the Serial Number

Every appliance has a serial number, and most manufacturers publish a decoder or will tell you the manufacture date if you call. Age is the variable the seller benefits from you not knowing.

What age tells you:

- **Remaining life.** A refrigerator is good for about 13 to 17 years, a washer 10 to 13, a dishwasher 9 to 12, a water heater 8 to 12. A five-year-old refurbished unit at 50% off is a much better bet than a twelve-year-old one at 60% off.
- **Whether parts exist.** Older units are often cheaper to repair and easier to find parts for — until they are not.
- **Efficiency.** An appliance built before the last efficiency standards can cost $50 to $150 a year more to run. Over its remaining life, that can erase the discount.

Ask for the serial number before you buy, in writing. A seller who will not provide it is telling you something.

## Inspection at Delivery

You have a short window to reject the unit — usually 48 hours to 30 days depending on the seller. Use it.

1. **Photograph the unit before it comes off the truck**, including the serial plate.
2. **Check the model number against the invoice.** Substitutions are common and sometimes convenient for the seller.
3. **Check for dents on every side**, and confirm disclosed damage matches what was described.
4. **Open and close every door, drawer, and lid.** Check the gaskets for tears and the hinges for sag.
5. **Run it.** Plug it in, run a cycle, let a refrigerator reach temperature for several hours before you put food in it. A refrigerator that does not reach 38°F within six hours has a problem.
6. **Check for water damage** — rust at the base, stains on the frame, a musty smell, corrosion on the bottom of the door.
7. **Confirm all parts are present.** Shelves, ice trays, drain hoses, mounting kits, and manuals are routinely missing from refurbished units and cost $50 to $200 to replace.
8. **Get the warranty document**, in writing, at the time of sale — not "it's in the box."

If something fails inspection, reject it in writing within the window. Do not accept a promise to fix it later.

## Total Cost of Ownership, Not Price

The comparison is not refurbished price versus new price. It is:

| Item | Refurbished | New |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase | $650 | $1,400 |
| Delivery and installation | $0 to $120 (sometimes excluded) | often included |
| Expected remaining life | 8 years | 13 years |
| Annual energy | $110 (older design) | $75 |
| Warranty coverage | 90 days parts and labour | 1 year + 5 to 10 years sealed system |
| Expected repair in 5 years | $300 to $500 (one board or pump) | under warranty |

Over eight years, the refurbished unit costs $650 + energy $880 + one repair $400 = $1,930. The new unit costs $1,400 + energy $975 + $0 = $2,375 over the same eight years, but it lasts five more years after that. Per year of service, the refurbished unit is roughly $240 and the new one roughly $180. The refurbished unit wins only if it genuinely lasts the eight years — which is exactly what the short warranty does not promise.

That is not an argument against refurbished. It is an argument for buying refurbished at the right discount, from the right seller, on the right appliance — the same total-cost framing as [repair or replace](/save-money/shopping/repair-or-replace-appliance/), applied before the purchase instead of after. At 50% off, with a one-year manufacturer warranty, on a simple machine, the arithmetic is strongly in your favour.

## Where to Buy

**Manufacturer outlet stores and certified refurbished programmes.** Best warranty, best parts, price fixed. Inventory is unpredictable, which is the trade-off.

**Large retailer open-box and outlet sections.** Full manufacturer warranty is common, and scratch-and-dent units are often mechanically new. Inspect in person if you can.

**Local refurbishers with a physical address and a service business.** Sometimes excellent, especially if they also do repairs and will service what they sell. Ask how long they have been at that address.

**Estate sales and moving sales.** Not refurbished, just used — but often the best value per dollar if you can test the unit and move it yourself.

**Online marketplaces.** The highest risk category: no return path, no warranty enforcement, and no way to verify condition. Fine for a $60 microwave, poor for a $900 refrigerator. The same judgment applies to [used furniture](/save-money/shopping/buy-used-furniture-safely/), where the checks are about structure and pests rather than compressors.

## When New Is Actually Cheaper

- **Rebates and utility programmes** on efficient new appliances can be $50 to $500, and they rarely apply to used units.
- **Delivery, installation, and haul-away** are frequently included with new purchases and cost $100 to $200 separately.
- **A long sealed-system warranty** on a new refrigerator has real value — the compressor is the expensive failure, and it is the one a refurbished warranty usually excludes.
- **Financing** at 0% on new appliances can beat paying cash for a refurbished unit if you would otherwise borrow.
- **Matching a kitchen** — a discontinued model in the right finish is worth something, and refurbished inventory cannot be specified.

## What NOT to Do

- **Do not buy refurbished without the warranty document in hand.** The badge is not the warranty.
- **Do not buy a refurbished refrigerator whose compressor was replaced** unless the work is documented and covered.
- **Do not buy sight-unseen from a marketplace seller** with no return policy.
- **Do not skip the serial number.** Age is the single most important fact and the easiest to hide.
- **Do not accept a "we'll fix it if anything comes up" verbal promise** instead of a written warranty.
- **Do not buy an old unit for the energy savings** — there are none; old appliances cost more to run.
- **Do not forget to measure the doorway, the stairwell, and the space** before delivery day.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is refurbished the same as used?**
No. Used means someone else owned it. Refurbished means a business inspected and usually repaired it, and the quality of that process is what the warranty reveals. A manufacturer-recertified unit is closer to new than to used.

**How long should a refurbished appliance warranty be?**
90 days is the common minimum and is thin. One year parts and labour is good. Anything on the sealed system is a bonus and worth asking about specifically.

**Can I buy an extended warranty on a refurbished appliance?**
Sometimes, but many third-party plans exclude refurbished units. Ask before you buy, because the extended plan is often the only protection after 90 days.

**Are scratch-and-dent appliances a better deal than refurbished?**
Often yes, if the damage is cosmetic and on a hidden side. They were usually new, so the mechanicals and the full manufacturer warranty are intact.

**Should I buy a refurbished refrigerator?**
Only with a warranty that covers the sealed system, or at a discount deep enough that replacing it in five years still beats buying new. It is the appliance where the downside is largest.

**What is a fair discount for refurbished?**
40% to 60% off the current new price. Below 25% off, the warranty difference is worth more than the saving.

**Does buying refurbished void the manufacturer warranty?**
A manufacturer-recertified unit carries its own warranty. A third-party refurbishment generally voids the original manufacturer warranty, which is why the refurbisher's own warranty is the only one you have.

## Bottom Line

Find out which of the four kinds of refurbished you are being sold, and read the warranty before the price tag. Manufacturer-recertified at 40% to 60% off is a good deal on a simple machine. A 90-day warranty on a refrigerator with an unknown compressor history is not a deal at any discount, because the failure you are protecting against is the one the warranty excludes.
