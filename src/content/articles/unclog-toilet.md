The first thing to do when a toilet won't flush isn't plunging. It's **stopping the water**, because the difference between a clog and a flooded bathroom is usually one more flush.

After that, the job is straightforward: most toilet clogs clear with a proper plunger used correctly. The "correctly" part matters — the common technique is wrong in a way that wastes most of the force.

---

## Step 0: Assess Before Acting

**What the water level tells you:**

| What you see | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Water level is normal, bowl won't drain | Standard clog | Plunge |
| Water rising toward the rim | Clog below the trap, risk of overflow | **Do not flush.** Shut off supply and plunge |
| Water draining slowly but going down | Partial blockage | Plunge, or wait and flush hot water |
| Multiple toilets or drains affected | Main line problem | Call a professional |
| Nothing at all happens when flushed | Could be a tank/flapper problem, not a clog | Check the tank before plunging |

**Check the tank quickly.** If the bowl isn't overfull and the flush is just weak, the problem may be the flapper, the chain, or the fill level rather than a clog. Lift the tank lid and watch — if the flapper isn't lifting fully, or the water level is low, you don't have a clog at all.

---

## Step 1: Stop the Water

**Turn the shutoff valve** behind or beside the toilet, usually near the floor on the wall side. Turn it clockwise until snug.

**If the bowl is close to overflowing,** this is now the priority, not the plunger. Stopping the supply prevents the tank refilling and adding more water to a bowl that can't drain.

**If you can't find the valve or it doesn't work,** remove the tank lid and lift the flapper manually to stop water entering the bowl — or close the main supply.

**Remove some water if the bowl is very full.** Use a cup and a bucket to bring the level down to roughly normal. This gives you room to work without spilling.

---

## Step 2: Use a Flange Plunger Correctly

**Use the right plunger.** A flange plunger has a soft rubber flap that folds out from the cup — that flap inserts into the toilet's outlet hole and creates the seal. A flat cup plunger cannot seal on a toilet outlet and will waste nearly all of your effort.

**The technique:**

1. **Soften the rubber** by running it under hot water for a few seconds — cold rubber doesn't seal
2. **Position the plunger** so the flange is inside the outlet hole, with the cup covering it
3. **Add water if needed** — the cup should be submerged. Air compresses; water doesn't
4. **Start with a gentle push** to expel air from the cup, then seal
5. **Push and pull with firm, controlled strokes.** The **pull** is as important as the push — it dislodges material and pulls water back through
6. **Keep the seal** throughout. If you hear air leaking, reposition
7. **Do 15–20 strokes,** then check. If the water drops, you've broken through
8. **Flush** to confirm — with the supply back on

**Why the common technique fails:** most people plunge with short, rapid jabs and never establish a seal. Without a seal, you're just moving water around the bowl.

---

## Step 3: If Plunging Doesn't Work — Hot Water and Dish Soap

This works well for soft blockages, especially paper, and it's worth trying before mechanical tools.

1. **Add a generous squirt of dish soap** into the bowl
2. **Pour a bucket of hot water** from roughly waist height — not boiling, and pour from a height for extra force
3. **Wait 20–30 minutes.** The soap lubricates and the warm water softens paper
4. **Try flushing.** Often the clog releases on its own

**Never use boiling water.** Thermal shock can crack the porcelain, and it can also soften the wax ring at the base.

---

## Step 4: Use a Toilet Auger

For clogs that resist plunging — usually a solid object — a toilet auger (also called a closet auger) is the right tool. It's designed with a protective sleeve so it won't scratch the porcelain.

1. **Insert the auger** into the bowl outlet with the cable retracted
2. **Crank slowly** while pushing forward. The bend is designed to navigate the trap
3. **When you feel resistance,** crank a few more turns to catch or break up the material
4. **Retract** and inspect what comes out
5. **Flush** to test

**Do not use a standard drain snake.** It will scratch the bowl. The auger's protective sleeve is the reason it exists.

**If you retrieve a solid object** — a toy, a toothbrush, a phone — you've solved the problem. Dispose of it and flush to confirm.

---

## If the Clog Is Past the Toilet

If plunging and augering both fail, the blockage is beyond the closet bend. At that point:

- **Stop.** Repeated aggressive plunging can loosen the wax seal at the base or crack the fixture
- **Call a plumber.** They have a machine that clears the line properly
- **Mention anything you've already tried,** especially any chemical product

**One option left:** removing the toilet. It's doable — shut off and disconnect the supply, remove the two floor bolts, and lift — but it's heavy, the wax ring usually needs replacing, and it's the point where most people are better off calling someone. If you do it, have a new wax ring on hand before you start.

If you cannot describe the failure modes out loud before starting, the job is yellow at best — [the line between a DIY fix and a service call](/fix-it/home-repair/diy-or-call-a-professional/) covers that rule.

## Never Put These in a Toilet

This is where prevention actually lives. The great majority of recurring toilet clogs are caused by one thing on this list.

| Item | Why it causes problems |
|---|---|
| "Flushable" wipes | **The single biggest cause.** They don't break down like paper; they stay intact and bind together |
| Paper towels and tissues | Designed to stay strong when wet — the opposite of toilet paper |
| Feminine hygiene products | Expand dramatically |
| Cotton pads, swabs, floss | Don't disintegrate; floss tangles |
| Dental floss | Wraps around other material and anchors clogs |
| Cat litter | Clumps and sets like concrete |
| Cigarette butts | Filters don't break down |
| Food and cooking grease | Solidifies in the line |
| Medications | Environmental problem as well as a clog risk |
| Hair | Binds with everything else |

**The wipes point deserves emphasis.** Products marketed as flushable typically pass a disintegration test in laboratory conditions that don't reflect residential plumbing. They're a leading cause of both household clogs and municipal sewer blockages. Bin them.

**The toilet paper test:** if it doesn't dissolve in water when you stir it in a glass, it doesn't belong in a toilet.

## Preventing Recurring Clogs

| Issue | Fix |
|---|---|
| Old, inefficient toilet | Modern units flush substantially more effectively |
| Low tank water level | Adjust the fill valve to the marked line |
| Partially closed shutoff valve | Open it fully |
| Weak flush from a worn flapper | Replace the flapper — it's cheap and takes minutes |
| Clogged rim holes under the bowl edge | Clean them with a small brush or a piece of wire |
| Mineral buildup in the trapway | Vinegar soak, or descaling product rated for toilets |

**Clean the rim holes.** Under the bowl rim, there's a ring of small holes that direct water into the bowl. In hard-water areas these scale up and dramatically weaken the flush. Cleaning them is a five-minute job that restores flush performance.

## Why This Works

**A plunger works by applying pressure differential across the clog, so sealing is everything.** Force applied to a bowl that can vent freely just moves water. The flange on a toilet plunger exists to close that vent — which is why a cup plunger, however vigorous, rarely works on a toilet.

**The pull stroke is mechanically as important as the push.** Push compresses the clog; pull draws it back toward the bowl and often breaks it apart. Plunging that only pushes is doing half the work.

**Most toilet clogs are physical objects in the trap, not buildup in the line.** Toilets have the largest trapway of any household fixture and are designed to pass waste and paper readily. When they block, it's usually something that shouldn't have been flushed — which is why prevention is so effective on this fixture specifically.

**Heat and surfactant soften paper-based clogs.** Dish soap lubricates and hot water softens, and gravity does the rest. It's slower than plunging but it works without mechanical risk.

## What NOT to Do

| Mistake | What happens | Do this instead |
|---|---|---|
| Flushing again to "see if it goes" | Overflow | Shut off the supply first |
| Using a cup plunger | No seal, wasted effort | Use a flange plunger |
| Plunging with air instead of water | Compresses, no force transfer | Submerge the cup |
| Only pushing, never pulling | Half the mechanism | Push and pull |
| Using boiling water | Cracks porcelain, softens the wax ring | Hot water, not boiling |
| Pouring chemical drain cleaner in a toilet | Can damage the fixture; creates a hazard for plumbers | Mechanical methods only |
| Using a regular drain snake | Scratches the bowl | Toilet auger |
| Forcing the auger hard | Can damage the trapway | Gentle cranking |
| Flushing wipes | The most common cause of repeat clogs | Bin them |

## Frequently Asked Questions
**Why won't my toilet unclog with a plunger?**
Most often the wrong plunger or no seal. Use a flange plunger, soften it in hot water, and make sure the cup is submerged.

**Can I use dish soap to unclog a toilet?**
Yes — it's effective for paper-based clogs. Add soap, pour hot (not boiling) water from a height, and wait 20–30 minutes.

**Is it safe to use drain cleaner in a toilet?**
Generally no. It can damage the fixture and creates a real hazard for anyone who later works on the line.

**How do I stop the toilet overflowing?**
Turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise. If that doesn't work, lift the tank lid and hold up the flapper.

**Why does my toilet keep clogging?**
Usually something non-dissolving is being flushed — most commonly wipes. If not, check the tank water level, the flapper, and the rim holes.

**Are flushable wipes really flushable?**
No. They are the leading cause of household and municipal blockages despite the label. Dispose of them in the trash.

**When should I call a plumber?**
When plunging and augering both fail, when multiple fixtures are affected, or when you suspect the clog is past the closet bend.

**Can a clogged toilet fix itself?**
Paper-based clogs sometimes clear on their own given time and hot water. Solid objects never do.
