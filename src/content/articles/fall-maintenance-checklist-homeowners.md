The fall checklist has a property no other seasonal list has: **a hard deadline you don't control.** Once the first hard freeze arrives, exterior caulk won't cure, concrete filler won't set, and gutter work becomes unsafe. Everything on the frozen-ground list has to be done before that date, whenever it happens to fall.

This is the fall-specific list, ordered by deadline rather than by category. General maintenance across all four seasons lives in [The Seasonal Home Maintenance Checklist](/fix-it/maintenance/seasonal-home-maintenance-checklist/); this one is about the fall window specifically.

---

## Why deadline order matters most in fall

Three of the four problem classes in a home are freeze-driven: burst pipes, ice dams, and freeze-thaw concrete damage. All three begin with water that wasn't diverted, and all three are prevented by tasks with a temperature limit.

So the list is sorted into three tiers:

| Tier | Meaning | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| **Must be done before first freeze** | Temperature-dependent or freeze-prevention | Complete while daytime temps stay above 50°F |
| **Before first hard freeze** | Safe to do in cold, but needed before the frost | Any time in fall |
| **Before first use** | Prep for equipment you'll rely on | Before you need it, not when you need it |

## Tier 1: Before the first freeze

These have a temperature deadline. If you do only six items all fall, do these six.

### 1. Clean the gutters and clear the downspouts

**Deadline:** after most leaves fall, before the first hard freeze.
**Protects:** foundation, fascia, siding, basement, roof edge.
**If skipped:** water backs up under the roof edge, saturates fascia, refreezes into an ice dam, and eventually enters the structure. This is the single most expensive preventable fall failure.

Remove debris by hand rather than flushing it into the downspouts, then flush each one with a hose and confirm flow at the outlet. Seal any leaking seams while the gutters are dry.

### 2. Caulk and weatherstrip everything

**Deadline:** before daytime temperatures drop below 50°F (10°C). Caulk below that simply doesn't cure and fails within one season.
**Protects:** heating cost, water intrusion, insect entry.
**If skipped:** you'll be doing it in spring, after a season of drafts and possibly water damage around the frames.

Priorities: exterior window and door frames, utility penetrations, and any joint where the old bead has cracked or pulled away. Replace door sweeps that no longer touch the threshold.

### 3. Fill concrete cracks in driveways, walks, and steps

**Deadline:** before the first freeze.
**Protects:** the concrete itself.
**If skipped:** water enters the crack, freezes, expands about 9 percent, and widens it. A hairline crack becomes a structural one after two winters.

### 4. Touch up exterior paint and seal exposed wood

**Deadline:** above 50°F for most products.
**Protects:** siding, trim, and any wood end-grain.
**If skipped:** water enters bare wood, freezes, and splits it — and rot follows.

### 5. Disconnect hoses and shut off exterior faucets

**Deadline:** before the first freeze.
**Protects:** the supply pipe inside the wall.
**If skipped:** a hose left connected traps water in the faucet body. That water freezes and splits the pipe inside the wall. You discover the leak when things thaw.

### 6. Drain the irrigation system

**Deadline:** before the first freeze.
**Protects:** irrigation lines, valves, and backflow preventer.
**If skipped:** buried lines crack and valves split — an expensive spring repair.

An inch of rain on a roof is hundreds of gallons — [fall gutter and yard cleanup](/seasonal/seasons/fall-yard-and-gutter-cleanup/) covers the drainage order.

## Tier 2: Before the first hard freeze

These are less temperature-sensitive but still needed before winter.

### Heating system

| Task | Protects |
|---|---|
| **Replace the furnace filter** | Airflow, efficiency, and system life — the cheapest item here |
| **Book a professional service** | Prevents a winter breakdown when repair demand peaks |
| **Test the system on a mild day** | Finds problems on your schedule instead of during a cold snap |
| **Vacuum vents and returns** | Air quality; dust recirculates through the house |
| **Check the flue for blockages** | CO safety |
| **Replace thermostat batteries; set a setback schedule** | The largest single heating saving available |

### Water and moisture

| Task | Protects |
|---|---|
| **Flush the water heater** | Removes sediment, improves efficiency, extends tank life |
| **Test the sump pump** | Basement flooding — snowmelt is a major risk |
| **Check the attic for previous water stains** | Tells you whether roof work is needed now |
| **Confirm grading slopes away from the foundation** | Basement and crawlspace moisture |
| **Seal rim joists in the basement** | Air leakage, comfort, pipes in that zone |

### Safety equipment

| Task | Protects |
|---|---|
| **Test smoke and CO detectors; replace batteries** | CO risk rises with heating season |
| **Have the chimney swept if you burn wood** | Fire safety — creosote is a real risk |
| **Check the snow removal equipment works** | You'll need it without warning |
| **Restock emergency supplies** | Storms, outages |

### Exterior and yard

| Task | Protects |
|---|---|
| **Rake leaves off the lawn** | Grass survival; a leaf mat smothers and molds |
| **Final mow, slightly shorter** | Reduces snow mold risk |
| **Fall fertilize the lawn** | Root reserves for winter survival — the most valuable feeding of the year |
| **Plant spring bulbs** | Before ground freezes |
| **Prune dead or overhanging branches** | Prevents ice and wind damage to the roof |
| **Move firewood away from the house and elevate it** | Termites, spiders, and beetles |
| **Store outdoor furniture and cushions** | Cushions must go indoors |
| **Drain and store garden hoses and equipment** | Freeze damage and next-spring reliability |
| **Service and store the mower** | Carburetor gumming; blade condition |
| **Bring in or protect tender plants** | Frost damage |

### Air sealing and insulation

| Task | Protects |
|---|---|
| **Seal attic air leaks and the attic hatch** | Heat loss — convective loss bypasses insulation |
| **Check the attic insulation level** | If you can see joists, add more |
| **Seal around recessed lights and penetrations** | Same as above |
| **Reverse ceiling fans to clockwise** | Moves warm air down from the ceiling |

## Tier 3: Before first use

| Task | When you'll need it |
|---|---|
| **Change the furnace filter** | First cold week |
| **Test the heating system** | First cold night |
| **Check the snow blower: fuel, belts, auger, spark plug** | First snowfall |
| **Locate and test the main water shutoff** | If a pipe bursts — knowing this saves thousands |
| **Confirm CO detectors work** | Any time the furnace or fireplace runs |
| **Clear the dryer vent** | Fire risk rises with heavier winter laundry |
| **Restock ice melt and shovels where you can reach them** | First ice |

Electric, gas, and heat pump dryers fail for entirely different reasons — [a dryer that tumbles but will not heat](/fix-it/appliances/dryer-not-heating/) checks the vent before the machine, because one of those failures is a fire risk.

## What this costs if you skip it

Ranked by the size of the repair relative to the effort of prevention:

| Skipped task | Typical consequence |
|---|---|
| Gutter cleaning | Ice dams, rotted fascia, water in the basement — structural repair |
| Disconnecting a hose | Split pipe inside the wall, drywall and insulation repair |
| Not draining irrigation | Cracked buried lines and split valves |
| Skipping air sealing | A season of avoidable heating cost |
| Not testing heat before winter | Emergency repair at peak demand prices |
| Not flushing the water heater | Shortened tank life, higher energy use |
| Leaving concrete cracks | Structural cracking after two freeze cycles |
| Not cleaning the dryer vent | Fire risk |

Every item in the top half of that table takes under an hour. Every consequence takes days and costs hundreds to thousands.

## A realistic schedule

If you can't do it all in one weekend:

- **A dry Saturday in early fall:** exterior caulking, paint touch-ups, concrete filler, hose disconnection. These are your temperature-deadline items.
- **A dry Saturday in mid-fall:** gutters, roof inspection, downspouts, grading.
- **A weekend before the first freeze:** furnace filter, heating test, water heater flush, detector tests, attic check.
- **Any wet indoor day:** air sealing, attic hatch, rim joist, ceiling fan direction.
- **A last warm day:** leaves, lawn, bulbs, furniture storage, equipment service.

## Why This Works

Fall is the only season where preparation is physically cheaper than repair, because winter adds a destructive mechanism that spring and summer don't have. Water expands about 9 percent on freezing, and in a confined space — a pipe, a crack, a gutter seam — that expansion generates enough force to split copper, concrete, and asphalt. Every fall task that keeps water out of a confined space prevents a failure mode that cannot occur in warm weather.

The temperature limits aren't arbitrary either. Caulk, paint, and concrete filler cure by chemical reaction, and reaction rates drop sharply with temperature. Below roughly 50°F, these products stop curing properly and fail within a season. This is why they belong in Tier 1 rather than being done whenever convenient.

Air sealing ahead of insulation follows from how heat leaves a house. Conductive loss through insulation scales with the temperature differential and is unavoidable. Convective loss moves air directly from indoors to outdoors through gaps, bypassing insulation entirely — a small unsealed opening can lose more heat than a large insulated surface retains. Sealing first is consistently the higher-return action.

Fall lawn fertilization matters more than spring for a physiological reason. In spring, grass is building top growth. In fall, it's storing carbohydrates in the roots, which is what determines winter survival and how early it greens up. Feeding into that storage phase builds reserves; feeding in spring mostly produces top growth that the first frost kills.

Testing equipment before you need it is about scheduling risk. Furnaces, snow blowers, and sump pumps fail on first use because that's when they're first loaded to capacity. Repair demand peaks at exactly that moment. Testing in advance surfaces problems when you have time, options, and normal prices.

## What NOT to Do

| Mistake | Why it fails | Do this instead |
|---|---|---|
| Doing exterior caulking after the first cold snap | Won't cure; fails within a season | Complete above 50°F |
| Flushing gutter debris into the downspouts | Creates a clog at the hardest point to reach | Remove by hand, then flush |
| Leaving downspouts discharging at the foundation | Water against the house → basement flooding | Extend 4 to 6 feet and fix the grade |
| Adding insulation without air sealing | Convective loss bypasses it | Seal first |
| Assuming the furnace is fine | Systems fail on first cold load | Test on a mild day first |
| Leaving the hose connected | Traps water in the bib; splits the pipe | Disconnect, drain, shut off inside |
| Skipping the water heater flush | Sediment reduces efficiency and shortens tank life | Flush annually |
| Storing cushions outside over winter | Foam core mildews and won't recover | Store indoors, dry and ventilated |
| Raking leaves onto the garden and leaving them matted | Smothers plants, harbors slugs | Remove from lawn; use a loose layer or compost |
| Covering the AC condenser but sealing it tight | Traps moisture | Use a breathable cover, or none |
| Pruning spring-flowering shrubs in fall | Removes next year's buds | Prune after spring bloom |
| Ignoring the sump pump until it rains | You discover the failure during the flood | Test by pouring water into the pit |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What fall maintenance must be done before the first freeze?**
Exterior caulking and painting, concrete crack filling, disconnecting and draining hoses, shutting off exterior faucets, and draining the irrigation system. These are the items with a hard temperature deadline.

**When is the best time to clean gutters in fall?**
After most leaves have fallen but before the first hard freeze. Too early and they refill; too late and debris is frozen in place. If you have heavy tree cover, expect to do it twice.

**Do I need to do fall maintenance if I did spring maintenance?**
Yes. Fall and spring cover different tasks. Fall is about preventing freeze damage and preparing for heating season; spring is about finding and repairing winter damage. Both are needed.

**What's the single most important fall task?**
Cleaning gutters and ensuring water drains away from the foundation. It's inexpensive, takes under an hour, and prevents the most expensive category of winter damage — ice dams, fascia rot, and basement water.

**Should I cover my AC unit for winter?**
A breathable cover is fine and keeps debris out. Don't seal it with plastic — the trapped moisture causes rust. Many units are fine uncovered.

**How do I know if I need more attic insulation?**
If you can see the floor joists when looking across the attic, you have less than the recommended depth for most cold climates. Air seal the attic first, then add insulation.

**Is fall lawn fertilization necessary?**
It's the most valuable feeding of the year in cold climates. Fall feeding supports root growth and carbohydrate storage, which determines winter survival and spring green-up.

**What should I do about the water heater in fall?**
Flush it to remove sediment, which improves efficiency and extends tank life. Also check for leaks and corrosion around the fittings and the anode rod if it's accessible.
